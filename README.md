# Advances in Quantization for Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models (2023 to 2025)

A white paper by **Aditya Saxena**, written in collaboration with UC Irvine Computer Science faculty, April to June 2025.

- **Read online:** https://asaxena9.github.io/llm-quantization-whitepaper/
- **PDF:** [Aditya_Saxena_LLM_Quantization_Whitepaper_2025.pdf](docs/Aditya_Saxena_LLM_Quantization_Whitepaper_2025.pdf) (12 pages, US Letter)
- **Source text:** [paper/whitepaper.md](paper/whitepaper.md)

## Abstract

Large language models and vision-language models are too large to serve cheaply, or to run on consumer and edge hardware, at full precision. This white paper reviews the post-training quantization methods published between 2023 and 2025 that made 4-bit weights, and 8-bit or even 4-bit activations, practical without full retraining: GPTQ, AWQ, OWQ, SpQR, SqueezeLLM, SmoothQuant and Atom, plus the first results on vision-language models. For each method it explains the core idea, the reported accuracy cost, and the memory and speed gains, and it closes with where quantization is used in edge and cloud deployment and what remains open, including quantization-aware training for large models.

## What the paper covers

1. Why quantization matters for LLMs and VLMs (memory, bandwidth and cost) and the trade-off between post-training quantization and quantization-aware training.
2. Post-training weight-only methods: GPTQ, AWQ, OWQ, SpQR and SqueezeLLM.
3. Quantizing weights and activations together: LLM.int8(), SmoothQuant and Atom, and why activation outliers are the hard part.
4. Quantization for vision-language models, including AWQ on multi-modal models and on-device VLM inference.
5. A side-by-side comparison of seven methods: precision, accuracy impact and notable results.
6. Real-world use in edge and cloud deployment, and the open problem of quantization-aware training for large models.

## Key findings, as reported by the cited papers

- **4-bit weights are close to lossless for 7B to 70B models.** GPTQ reports about 3.25 times faster inference than FP16 on an A100. AWQ with its TinyChat engine reports more than 3 times faster generation and runs a 70B Llama-2 on a 64 GB Jetson Orin.
- **Handling outlier weights buys the last bits.** OWQ's 3.1-bit models match 4-bit GPTQ. SpQR stays within 1% perplexity of the full model with more than 4 times less memory and about 15% faster inference. SqueezeLLM's 3-bit models run 2.3 times faster than FP16 on an A6000.
- **Quantizing activations needs outlier smoothing.** SmoothQuant's W8A8 gives a 1.56 times speedup and 2 times less memory with negligible accuracy loss. Atom's W4A4 reports up to 7.7 times higher throughput with a slight accuracy loss.
- **The same methods carry over to vision-language models.** AWQ was the first method shown to work on multi-modal LLMs without degradation, which makes captioning and visual question answering possible on edge devices.
- **Quantization-aware training is the main open direction.** Brief fine-tuning recovers most of the loss at 8 bits, while 4-bit QAT remains costly and hard.

## Methods compared

| Method | Venue | Precision | Headline result |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPTQ | ICLR 2023 | 3 to 4 bit weights | 175B model on a single GPU with near-original accuracy; more than 3 times faster than FP16 on an A100 |
| AWQ | MLSys 2024 | 4 bit weights | Better perplexity than GPTQ from 7B to 70B; first method shown to work on multi-modal LLMs |
| OWQ | AAAI 2024 | about 3.1 bit, mixed | 3.1-bit accuracy equal to 4-bit GPTQ; weak column tuning for cheap task adaptation |
| SpQR | arXiv 2023 | 3 to 4 bit weights plus sparse outliers | Under 1% perplexity loss; 33B model on a single 24 GB GPU |
| SqueezeLLM | ICML 2024 | 3 bit average | Perplexity gap to FP16 cut by up to 2.1 times; 2.3 times faster on an A6000 |
| SmoothQuant | ICML 2023 | 8 bit weights and activations | INT8 for all layers via activation smoothing; 1.56 times faster, 2 times less memory |
| Atom | MLSys 2024 | 4 bit weights and activations | Near-FP16 accuracy at 4 bits; up to 7.7 times higher serving throughput |
